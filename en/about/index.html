About this site

The companion to Category Theory for Mathematical Physics — braided, fusion and module categories, in pictures.

What is here

14 lectures with 43 definitions, 81 numbered results, 58 proofs, 25 worked examples and 106 figures, and 85 problems on 14 exercise sheets so far. Everything is generated from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes: a theorem here is the same object, with the same number, as the theorem in the PDF, and every number on the site is checked against the typeset volume before the site is built.

The course is fourteen lectures in six blocks: the language, categories, monoidal categories and duals, drawn as string diagrams; anyons, fusion, F-symbols and braiding; quantum groups and knots, a side branch; topological order, modular tensor categories; boundaries and defects, module categories and the Drinfeld centre; and a synthesis in topological quantum field theory. Optional lectures, when written, follow Lecture 14 without renumbering it.

How much is proved

The notes are honest about rigour. A result with no tag is proved in full. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used, and its proof lives in the references. A result tagged physical principle is a principle of physics, stated as such and never as a theorem. The notes develop the main arguments; extended practice is on the exercise sheets.

Questions and solutions

Each exercise has a hint, an answer and a complete worked method. Attempt the question, reveal a hint if needed, and compare the worked solution after making your own attempt. The feedback starts collapsed.

Practice sheets are available in English and Lithuanian from their language editions. Each sheet has three clearly named PDF editions: questions only, hints and answers, and worked solutions. The prerequisite diagnostic and the reading guide help you choose what to revisit.

The lecture self-checks provide explained feedback online. The self-check companion PDF contains all the same questions, hints, answers and explanations for use alongside the notes.

Your privacy

Your optional Matfiz profile and learning progress stay in this browser. Courses on the same site share this profile. Nothing is sent to a server. Use My progress to download a backup, restore it on another device, or remove your learning records.

Found a mistake?

Please report the location and what went wrong to the course author. Corrections are made in the shared source and carried to both editions.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source and checked against the typeset volume; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the style file the PDFs load; the string diagrams and commutative diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF and the data figures the same generated plots, converted to SVG. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
