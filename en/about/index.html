About this site

The companion to Category Theory for Mathematical Physics — braided, fusion and module categories, in pictures.

What is here

14 lectures with 43 definitions, 81 numbered results, 58 proofs, 25 worked examples and 89 figures, and 0 problems on 0 exercise sheets so far. Everything is generated from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes: a theorem here is the same object, with the same number, as the theorem in the PDF, and every number on the site is checked against the typeset volume before the site is built.

The course is fourteen lectures in six blocks: the language, categories, monoidal categories and duals, drawn as string diagrams; anyons, fusion, F-symbols and braiding; quantum groups and knots, a side branch; topological order, modular tensor categories; boundaries and defects, module categories and the Drinfeld centre; and a synthesis in topological quantum field theory. Optional lectures, when written, follow Lecture 14 without renumbering it.

How much is proved

The notes are honest about rigour. A result with no tag is proved in full. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used, and its proof lives in the references. A result tagged physical principle is a principle of physics, stated as such and never as a theorem. The notes are the core that is taught; everything else is on the exercise sheets.

Questions and solutions

An exercise is a question, and a solution is the full worked method. This site is always the questions-only edition: worked solutions are done in the tutorial, which is where the learning in them actually happens.

They are not merely hidden here. Every solution is deleted from the source text before it reaches the converter, the public sheet PDFs are recompiled as the questions-only variant rather than copied from the repository, and the finished site and every PDF in it are searched for solution material before anything can be published.

Found a mistake?

Please report it. Corrections during a course's first run are genuinely valuable — bring it to a lecture or tutorial and it will be fixed at source and republished.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source and checked against the typeset volume; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the style file the PDFs load; the string diagrams and commutative diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF and the data figures the same generated plots, converted to SVG. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
