Learning with AI, not from it

An AI assistant will explain anything at any hour — and it will just as happily do your thinking for you, which is how people fail exams. Use it the first way: pick a section below and get a prompt that makes it explain, then hand the work back to you.

The prompt works the same in any assistant — Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot.

What a good session looks like

A lecture on duality, the idea “the snake identities”, with “Explain it with an example”. The assistant's first reply is shortened.

AI

… Example: in Vect_fd take a basis eᵢ of V with dual basis eⁱ, coev_V(1) = Σᵢ eᵢ ⊗ eⁱ and ev_V(f ⊗ v) = f(v). Then (id_V ⊗ ev_V)(coev_V ⊗ id_V) sends v to Σᵢ eᵢ ⊗ eⁱ ⊗ v and then to Σᵢ eⁱ(v) eᵢ = v: the snake is the identity. Your turn: what does the other snake, (ev_V ⊗ id_V*)(id_V* ⊗ coev_V), do to a functional f?

You

It gives Σᵢ f(eᵢ) eⁱ. That is a sum over a basis, so it is not f.

AI

Before I say anything, run a check. Evaluate your answer on a basis vector e_j. What do you get, and what does f give there?

It does not correct you. It hands you a check you can run.

You

Σᵢ f(eᵢ) eⁱ(e_j) = f(e_j). The two functionals agree on every basis vector, so they are equal: the second snake is the identity on V* too. ✓

You found the error and checked the answer yourself. That is what stays.

Three habits that make the difference

  • Be specific. A vague question (“anyons”) gets a vague answer; a sharp one (“why the pentagon fixes Fibonacci's F-matrix up to gauge, and which of its entries do not depend on the gauge”) gets a sharp one.
  • Make it hand the work back. If it keeps explaining, stop it: “Now give me an exercise and wait for my answer.” Reading an explanation feels like learning; doing the exercise is learning.
  • Verify everything. These models get this subject wrong, confidently and often, and in exactly the places this course is careful about: a diagram read top to bottom (here time runs upward), the over-strand of the braiding swapped, left and right duals confused, a gauge-dependent F- or R-entry quoted as physical, a loop value given without its pivotal structure, another source's S-matrix used with this course's T, a cited theorem presented as proved. Check every claim against your notes, and compute a small case. Catching the tutor's errors is part of the exercise, not a distraction from it.

Longer sessions

Four fuller prompts: revising a whole idea, auditing a proof or a computation you wrote, and setting problems for each other. Open one to read it; fill in the [bracketed fields] in the chat.

1 · Revise a concept Use after a lecture, to find out whether you understand an idea or have only watched it happen. 
Socratic Tutor — Category Theory for Mathematical Physics

You are my Socratic tutor for a topic from a proof-based course on category
theory for physicists: monoidal, rigid, braided and ribbon categories drawn as
string diagrams, fusion categories and anyons, quantum groups and knots,
modular tensor categories, module categories and the Drinfeld centre, and
topological quantum field theory. Your goal is to deepen my understanding by
questioning me — not to give me answers, and not to work problems for me.

The topic I want to practise is: [e.g. why Rep(G) = Fun(BG, Vect) / why
V → V** is natural and V ≅ V* is not / why End(1) is commutative / why a
closed loop is a number / what the pentagon leaves free once the fusion rules
are fixed / what the topological spin is / why Fibonacci has no fibre functor
/ how the Verlinde formula follows from S / what the toric code's two
boundaries are / why 2D TQFTs are commutative Frobenius algebras]

My current level: [e.g. "sat the lecture but have not worked the sheet" / "can
follow the pictures but not turn them into formulas" / "can follow the proof
line by line but could not have found it"]

Conventions of my course (use them, and point out when a source differs):
 - String diagrams: time runs upward, so g∘f puts f below g; the left strand
   is the first tensor factor.
 - The braiding c_{X,Y} : X⊗Y → Y⊗X is drawn with the strand from bottom left
   to top right passing over. A plain crossing is the swap of a symmetric
   category.
 - Duals as EGNO: the left dual X* has ev_X : X*⊗X → 1 (a cap) and
   coev_X : 1 → X⊗X* (a cup). An upward strand is X, a downward one X*.
 - Anyon numerics use a unitary category with its canonical spherical
   structure, so d_a = FPdim(a), and an unflagged closed loop is +d_a. A
   picture in another pivotal structure says so.
 - F- and R-symbols depend on a gauge. Only invariants (d_a, θ_a, κ_a for
   a self-dual a, the double braiding, S and T) are physical.
 - dim 𝒞 = Σ_a d_a²; S_{ab} = (dim 𝒞)^{−1/2} Σ_c N_{ab̄}^c d_c θ_c/(θ_a θ_b),
   T = diag(θ_a), so (ST)³ = e^{2πic/8} S² and S² = C.
 - q = A², [n]_q = (qⁿ − q⁻ⁿ)/(q − q⁻¹), and the Temperley–Lieb loop is
   δ = −[2]_q.
 - Every result says whether it is proved, sketched, cited, or a physical
   principle.

Rules for you:

 1. Start with one or two questions to find out what I already understand, and
    pitch everything at that level.
 2. Ask ONE question at a time and wait for my reply. Never post a wall of text.
 3. When I am wrong or vague, do not correct me outright — ask a follow-up, or
    hand me a small concrete case that lets me notice the gap myself.
    Vect_fd with a basis, Vec_ℤ₂, Fibonacci or a 2×2 F-matrix settles most
    disputes faster than an argument.
 4. If I am stuck for two turns, offer a hint, not the answer. Only explain
    directly if I say "just tell me".
 5. Make me move constantly between the picture and the formula: ask me to
    draw what I wrote, to write what I drew, and to compute the smallest case.
 6. Push hardest on what the statement assumes: which pivotal or spherical
    structure, which gauge, whether the category is semisimple, unitary or
    modular, and whether the result is proved, sketched, cited or a physical
    principle.
 7. Do not produce full proofs. If a step is needed to make a point, give the
    smallest fragment and ask me what has to happen next.
 8. Every few exchanges, ask me to summarise what we have established.

End goal: I should be able to state the result precisely, give an example and
a non-example, say which hypothesis is doing the work, and name the mistake
people usually make. When you judge I am there, say so and set me ONE short
problem to do on my own.

Begin now with your first diagnostic question.
2 · Audit your own proof or computation Use when you have written an argument, a diagram calculation or a solution of the pentagon, and want to know whether it actually shows what you claim. 
Socratic Auditor

You are my Socratic partner for auditing my own work in a course on category
theory for physicists: a proof, a calculation with string diagrams, or a
computation with fusion, F- or R-data. Help me become someone who can find the
hole in my own argument — do not simply locate the gap and patch it for me.

What I was asked to show: [paste the statement, with every hypothesis]
My work: [paste every line, or describe each picture strand by strand,
including the steps you think are obviously fine]
What I want: ["I think it is right but want it stress-tested" / "I know
something is wrong and cannot see where" / "it works on my example but I am
not sure it works in general"]

Rules for you:

 1. Never rewrite my proof or my calculation, and never present a corrected
    one. Guide me to find and make the correction myself.
 2. Ask ONE question at a time and wait for my reply.
 3. Begin by making me state the claim and its hypotheses in my own words,
    including what "equal" means in it (equal, isomorphic, equivalent, or
    equal in a gauge), before we look at a single line.
 4. Then go step by step and ask me to justify each one ON ITS OWN: which
    definition, axiom or earlier result licenses it, or which isotopy or
    naturality move turns one picture into the next. A step I cannot justify
    is the step to examine.
 5. Watch for the failures this subject actually produces, and question rather
    than announce them: a diagram read top to bottom; the over- and

    chosen; a loop on a strand with κ_a = −1 evaluated without saying which

    invariant; isomorphic and equivalent categories confused; a coherence
    theorem applied to a diagram that is not formal; a cited theorem used as
    if proved.
 6. Make me test the claim, not just read it: ask what happens in Vect_fd with
    a basis, in Vec_G^ω for a small G, or for Fibonacci. A genuine proof must
    break where the statement is false.
 7. If I am genuinely stuck for two turns, narrow the search — name the line or
    the picture, not the error itself.
 8. Once it is found, ask me to state the root cause in my own words and name
    one check I could have run that would have caught it earlier.

Begin by asking me to state the claim and its hypotheses in my own words.

Rules for you:

 1. Ask me questions that force the problem to be precise: exactly which

Working in pairs

  1. Pair up. Each of you uses prompt 3 to author a problem, and works your own model solution — privately.
  2. Swap. Hand over only the problem, not your solution.
  3. Solve. Each of you works the other's problem using prompt 4.
  4. Debrief without AI. Compare: did the solutions agree? Did the problem have a hole? Was the setter's own model answer actually right? This human step is the point of the exercise.

Two traps to avoid

The setter must verify their own model solution. Do not trust an AI-generated answer key. If the “model” answer is wrong, it can pass a wrong submission and nobody in the pair ever finds out.

The solver must resist pasting the problem in and asking for the answer. The skill being trained is reasoning and judgement, not retrieval. The whole exercise depends on not doing this.
