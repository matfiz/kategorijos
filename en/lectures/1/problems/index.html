  1. Exercise 1 Categories, non-examples, and groups

    (i) Take as objects the natural numbers 0,1,2,…0,1,2,\dots0,1,2,…, with exactly one morphism m→nm\to nm→n when m≤nm\le nm≤n and none otherwise. Show that this is a category, and find its isomorphisms.

    (ii) Take as objects the finite-dimensional complex vector spaces, and as morphisms V→WV\to WV→W the linear maps of rank at most 111, composed as maps. Show that a composite of two such maps is again one. Show that this is not a category, and find the objects VVV that have an identity morphism.

    (iii) Show that a functor BG→BHBG\to BHBG→BH is exactly a homomorphism G→HG\to HG→H. How many functors are there BZ4→BZ2B\Z_4\to B\Z_2BZ4​→BZ2​, and how many BZ2→BZ3B\Z_2\to B\Z_3BZ2​→BZ3​?

    (iv) Let φ,ψ ⁣:G→H\varphi,\psi\colon G\to Hφ,ψ:G→H be homomorphisms, read as functors BG→BHBG\to BHBG→BH.     In (i) and (iii) each axiom becomes a familiar property: of ≤\le≤ in (i), of a homomorphism in (iii).     Show the answer

    (i) A category; its only isomorphisms are the identities. (ii) Composites have rank at most 111, but an object VVV has an identity exactly when dim⁡V≤1\dim V\le1dimV≤1, so it is not a category. (iii) 222 functors BZ4→BZ2B\Z_4\to B\Z_2BZ4​→BZ2​ and 111 functor BZ2→BZ3B\Z_2\to B\Z_3BZ2​→BZ3​. (iv) 333 classes: the trivial homomorphism, the three onto a subgroup of order 222, and the six automorphisms.

    Show the worked method

    (i) Write m→nm\to nm→n for the unique morphism when m≤nm\le nm≤n. If m≤nm\le nm≤n and n≤pn\le pn≤p then m≤pm\le pm≤p, so the composite exists and is the unique morphism m→pm\to pm→p; m≤mm\le mm≤m gives the identity. Every Hom⁡\HomHom set has at most one element, so any two morphisms with the same source and target are equal: associativity and the unit laws hold automatically. An isomorphism m→nm\to nm→n needs a morphism n→mn\to mn→m as well, so m≤n≤mm\le n\le mm≤n≤m and m=nm=nm=n: only the identities are isomorphisms.

    (ii) If f ⁣:U→Vf\colon U\to Vf:U→V has rank at most 111, then im⁡(g∘f)=g(im⁡f)\operatorname{im}(g\circ f) =g(\operatorname{im}f)im(g∘f)=g(imf) has dimension at most dim⁡im⁡f≤1\dim\operatorname{im}f\le1dimimf≤1, for every linear ggg. Composition of maps is associative. Now suppose e ⁣:V→Ve\colon V\to Ve:V→V is an identity for VVV in this collection. For each nonzero v∈Vv\in Vv∈V take the rank-one map f ⁣:C→Vf\colon\C\to Vf:C→V, f(1)=vf(1)=vf(1)=v; then e∘f=fe\circ f=fe∘f=f gives e(v)=ve(v)=ve(v)=v. So e=id⁡Ve=\id_Ve=idV​, whose rank is dim⁡V\dim VdimV: an identity exists exactly when dim⁡V≤1\dim V\le1dimV≤1 (for dim⁡V=0,1\dim V=0,1dimV=0,1, id⁡V\id_VidV​ itself has rank at most 111). Since C2\C^{2}C2 is an object with no identity, this is not a category.

    (iii) As in the proof of Theorem 1.9, a functor F ⁣:BG→BHF\colon BG\to BHF:BG→BH sends ∗\ast∗ to ∗\ast∗ and each ggg to F(g)∈HF(g)\in HF(g)∈H, with F(gh)=F(g)F(h)F(gh)=F(g)F(h)F(gh)=F(g)F(h) and F(1)=1F(1)=1F(1)=1: a homomorphism. In Z2\Z_2Z2​ both elements satisfy 4x=04x=04x=0, so there are 222 functors BZ4→BZ2B\Z_4\to B\Z_2BZ4​→BZ2​; in Z3\Z_3Z3​ only 000 satisfies 2y=02y=02y=0, so there is 111 functor BZ2→BZ3B\Z_2\to B\Z_3BZ2​→BZ3​.

    (iv) A natural transformation has one component h=ξ∗∈Hh=\xi_\ast\in Hh=ξ∗​∈H, and naturality for the morphism ggg reads ψ(g)∘h=h∘φ(g)\psi(g)\circ h=h\circ\varphi(g)ψ(g)∘h=h∘φ(g), that is ψ(g)=hφ(g)h−1\psi(g)=h\varphi(g)h^{-1}ψ(g)=hφ(g)h−1. For S3→S3S_3\to S_3S3​→S3​, sort by the kernel, a normal subgroup: S3S_3S3​, A3A_3A3​ or {1}\{1\}{1}. Kernel S3S_3S3​: the trivial homomorphism, 111 of them. Kernel A3A_3A3​: the image has order 222, generated by one of the three transpositions; the three are conjugate. Kernel {1}\{1\}{1}: the automorphisms of S3S_3S3​.
