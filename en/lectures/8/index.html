Lecture 8

Hopf algebras and quantum groups

What this lecture does

Lecture 7 found braidings by solving equations. Where do they come from? For groups, the tensor product of representations comes from the diagonal g↦g⊗gg\mapsto g\otimes gg↦g⊗g, duals from g↦g−1g\mapsto g^{-1}g↦g−1, and the symmetry from the flip. This lecture asks which algebras HHH give a monoidal, rigid and braided Rep(H)\Rep(H)Rep(H), and proves that bialgebras, Hopf algebras and quasitriangular Hopf algebras suffice. The quantum group Uq(sl2)U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)Uq​(sl2​) then produces a braiding that is not a symmetry, and its matrix on two spins is, after a change of gauge, the braid limit of Integrability’s XXZ R-matrix. This is a side branch: Lecture 9 needs only that matrix.

8.1 Bialgebras give monoidal representation categories

All modules here are finite-dimensional. The algebras acting on them may be infinite-dimensional, as for U(g)U(\mathfrak g)U(g) and UqU_qUq​. The tensor product of linear maps is the ordinary one. An algebra is (H,μ,ι)(H,\mu,\iota)(H,μ,ι), with multiplication μ ⁣:H⊗H→H\mu\colon H\otimes H\to Hμ:H⊗H→H and unit ι ⁣:C→H\iota\colon\C\to Hι:C→H, associative and unital. In pictures, read upward, μ\muμ and Δ\DeltaΔ are a fusion and a splitting vertex: Kassel writes η\etaη for the unit and plain ε\varepsilonε for the counit; here η\etaη and ε\varepsilonε are taken.

Definition 8.1 (Bialgebra). 8.4 Reconstruction, and what it forbids

A fibre functor is a faithful C\CC-linear monoidal functor to Vect\VectVect, like the forgetful functor of Rep(H)\Rep(H)Rep(H) (EGNO Def. 5.1.1; on a semisimple category the exactness EGNO asks for is automatic).

Theorem 8.8 (Reconstruction cited). A fusion category with a fibre functor is equivalent to Rep(H)\Rep(H)Rep(H) for a finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebra HHH, built from the endomorphisms of the functor (EGNO Thm. 5.3.12, applied to fusion categories).

A fibre functor preserves Frobenius–Perron dimensions (EGNO Prop. 4.5.7), and in Vect\VectVect they are dimensions, integers. Fibonacci has FPdim(τ)=φ\mathrm{FPdim}(\tau)=\varphiFPdim(τ)=φ and Ising FPdim(σ)=2\mathrm{FPdim}(\sigma)=\sqrt2FPdim(σ)=2​, so neither has a fibre functor: no Hopf algebra has Fibonacci or Ising as its representations. Integer FPdims are necessary, not sufficient: VecGω\VecG[\omega]VecGω​ with ω\omegaω not a coboundary has every FPdim 111 and no fibre functor (EGNO Ex. 5.1.3), since a fibre functor would trivialise ω\omegaω. The obstruction is FPdim, not the pivotal ddd: VecG\VecGVecG​ for G=Z3G=\Z_3G=Z3​ has the fibre functor that forgets the grading, yet a pivotal structure with d=e2πi/3d=e^{2\pi i/3}d=e2πi/3 (Lecture 4). The Fibonacci category does come from a quantum group, but as a subcategory of a quotient of its representations at a root of unity, the subject of Lecture 9.

Summary

  • Handed on: a coproduct combines two systems; a bialgebra makes Rep(H)\Rep(H)Rep(H) monoidal (Theorem 8.2), an antipode makes it rigid (Proposition 8.5), a universal R-matrix makes it braided, with c=P∘Rc=P\circ Rc=P∘R (Theorem 8.7).
  • Reconstruction: a fibre functor makes a fusion category Rep(H)\Rep(H)Rep(H) (Theorem 8.8); integer FPdims are necessary, so Fibonacci and Ising are not Rep(H)\Rep(H)Rep(H).
  • The quantum group: on two spins, c=A−1Bqc=A^{-1}B_qc=A−1Bq​ with (Bq−q)(Bq+q−1)=0(B_q-q)(B_q+q^{-1})=0(Bq​−q)(Bq​+q−1)=0 (Theorem 8.9); BqB_qBq​ is, after a change of gauge, the braid limit of the XXZ R-matrix.
  • Next: Lecture 9 turns BqB_qBq​ into the Temperley–Lieb and Kauffman-bracket calculus, adds the twist, and builds SU(2)k\SU(2)_kSU(2)k​.
