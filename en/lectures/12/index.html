Lecture 12

Morita equivalence and the Drinfeld centre

What this lecture does

A fusion category C\cat{C}C acting on a module category M\cat{M}M (Lecture 11) has a second symmetry category, its dual, whose objects are the module endofunctors of M\cat{M}M. For VecG\VecGVecG​ acting on Vectfd\VectfdVectfd​ the dual is Rep(G)\Rep(G)Rep(G). These categories have equivalent Drinfeld centres. An object of the centre Z(C)\mathcal{Z}(\cat{C})Z(C) is an object of C\cat{C}C equipped with a compatible family of crossings with every other object. The chosen crossings are additional data. The centre is braided, and modular when C\cat{C}C is spherical. For VecG\VecGVecG​ with G=Z2G=\Z_2G=Z2​, its anyons are those of the toric code of Quantum Computing.

Definition 12.4 (Deligne product, bimodule category). Definition 12.5 (Morita equivalence). Fusion categories C\cat{C}C and D\cat{D}D are Morita equivalent if there is an indecomposable semisimple C\cat{C}C-module category M\cat{M}M with D⊗op≃CM∗\cat{D}^{\otimes\mathrm{op}}\simeq\cat{C}^{*}_{\cat{M}}D⊗op≃CM∗​ as tensor categories (EGNO Def. 7.12.17). Then M\cat{M}M is a (C,D)(\cat{C},\cat{D})(C,D)-bimodule category.

Without ⊗op{\otimes\mathrm{op}}⊗op the centres of §12.4 would match only up to reversing the braiding. By Theorem 12.2, VecG\VecGVecG​ and Rep(G)\Rep(G)Rep(G) are Morita equivalent, since Rep(G)⊗op≃Rep(G)\Rep(G)^{\otimes\mathrm{op}}\simeq\Rep(G)Rep(G)⊗op≃Rep(G) by the flip. Physically, Morita equivalent symmetry categories describe the same two-dimensional bulk, seen through different boundaries physical principle (Kitaev and Kong; Lecture 13).

Theorem 12.11 (Etingof, Nikshych and Ostrik cited). Fusion categories C\cat{C}C and D\cat{D}D are Morita equivalent if and only if Z(C)≃Z(D)\mathcal{Z}(\cat{C})\simeq\mathcal{Z}(\cat{D})Z(C)≃Z(D) as braided categories (EGNO Thm. 8.12.3).

So Z(VecG)≃Z(RepG)\mathcal{Z}(\VecG)\simeq\mathcal{Z}(\Rep G)Z(VecG​)≃Z(RepG) is Morita equivalence seen from the centre, and VecZ2\catname{Vec}_{\Z_2}VecZ2​​ and its twist by ω\omegaω are not Morita equivalent: the double semion has spins ±i\pm i±i, the toric code none. Physically, a gapped two-dimensional phase whose boundary carries the symmetry C\cat{C}C has the anyons of Z(C)\mathcal{Z}(\cat{C})Z(C) in its bulk, and every indecomposable module category is a gapped boundary of it physical principle (Kitaev and Kong, §4; the bulk of a string net is the centre, Simon p. 485): the toric code is the bulk of VecZ2\catname{Vec}_{\Z_2}VecZ2​​, whose two module categories are its two boundaries, and Morita equivalent symmetries share a bulk. Lecture 13 makes this precise on the lattice.
