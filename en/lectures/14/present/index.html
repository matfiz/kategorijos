Lecture 14

Topological quantum field theory: a synthesis

The course has built duals (Lecture 4), F-symbols (Lecture 6), modular data (Lecture 10), algebras (Lecture 11) and centres (Lecture 12). A topological quantum field theory assigns vector spaces to spaces and linear maps to spacetimes between them. In one dimension it is determined by a dual pair. In two dimensions it is determined by a commutative Frobenius algebra. Finite groups give examples called Dijkgraaf–Witten theories. In three dimensions a spherical fusion category gives a Turaev–Viro state sum. For a unitary category its Hamiltonian form is a string net. A modular category gives the Reshetikhin–Turaev theory, related to the state sum through the centre. These constructions cover selected structures of the course. Turaev–Viro theories are non-chiral; Rep(SU(2))\Rep(\SU(2))Rep(SU(2)) and generic UqU_qUq​ are not finite inputs.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Define a TQFT as a symmetric monoidal functor, and prove that its state spaces are finite-dimensional, with Z(Σ×S1)=dim⁡Z(Σ)Z(\Sigma\times S^{1})=\dim Z(\Sigma)Z(Σ×S1)=dimZ(Σ).
  • Define Frobenius algebras in pictures, state the classification of 2D TQFTs, and compute partition functions with the handle element.
  • Compute 2D Dijkgraaf–Witten partition functions by Mednykh’s formula.
  • State the Turaev–Viro and Reshetikhin–Turaev constructions and their relation through the centre, with the framing anomaly, and count torus states.

14.1 Atiyah’s axioms; 1D

The bordism category Bordn\mathrm{Bord}_nBordn​ has as objects closed oriented (n−1)(n-1)(n−1)-manifolds, and as morphisms Σ→Σ′\Sigma\to\Sigma'Σ→Σ′ the compact oriented nnn-manifolds MMM with ∂M=Σˉ⊔Σ′\partial M=\bar\Sigma\sqcup\Sigma'∂M=Σˉ⊔Σ′, up to diffeomorphism fixing the boundary. Definition 14.1 (TQFT). An nnn-dimensional topological quantum field theory is a symmetric monoidal functor Z ⁣:Bordn→VectZ\colon\mathrm{Bord}_n\to\VectZ:Bordn​→Vect (Kock 1.3.32; Carqueville–Runkel Def. 2.1).

The dimension of the bordism decides what kind of value ZZZ assigns. A surface is a spacetime in 2D and a space in 3D.

14.2 Frobenius algebras; 2D TQFT is commutative Frobenius

In two dimensions the objects are disjoint unions of circles, and every surface with boundary is glued from a few pieces:

the pair of pants, read upward, merges two circles into one; turned over it splits one into two; the disc with its circle on top creates a circle from nothing; the disc with its circle below annihilates one. 14.3 Dijkgraaf–Witten

Deforming a path without crossing a hole does not change its label in a flat assignment. The group π1Σg\pi_1\Sigma_gπ1​Σg​ records closed paths from a chosen base point, with deformations identified and multiplication given by following paths. From processes to computable invariants

In Lecture 1 the category Cob2\Cob_2Cob2​ described circles and surfaces as systems and processes, but no numerical value had been assigned to a surface. We can now choose a commutative Frobenius algebra, turn each disc and pair of pants into a typed linear map, and compute the value of a glued surface. The calculation for A=C2A=\C^2A=C2 above gives such a functor explicitly on its generators and evaluates its sphere, torus and genus-two surface. The cited presentation ensures that a different decomposition gives the same map.

The three-dimensional constructions use more of the course. Fusion trees describe state spaces, F-symbols change their bases, and the pentagon relates the local tetrahedral moves. The centre identifies the bulk anyons of a string net; the module-category calculation distinguishes boundaries that absorb different charges. For S3S_3S3​, the value 333 of a closed torus in the 2D theory and the dimension 888 of the torus state space in the 3D theory answer different typed questions. The finite calculations are explicit; the general TV/RT and bulk–boundary constructions retain the cited or sketched status stated above. These are concrete uses of the objects, morphisms and functors with which the course began.

Optional outlook

For further study, a fully extended TQFT assigns data in every lower dimension, down to points. Framed theories also retain a continuously chosen frame of tangent vectors. The cobordism hypothesis classifies such theories by a higher-dimensional duality condition called full dualizability (Lurie, proof outline, Thm. 1.2.16). Fusion categories over C\CC satisfy this condition and give framed 3D theories with a fusion category assigned to a point (Douglas, Schommer-Pries and Snyder, Thm. 1). No result here uses this classification.
