Lecture 7

Braiding: hexagons, Yang–Baxter and topological spin

Lectures 5 and 6 built fusion categories, with their particles and associators. In three dimensions, exchanging two identical particles twice is the identity, and exchange statistics are those of the symmetric group: bosons and fermions. In a plane the two exchanges are not the same, and the symmetric group is replaced by the braid group. This lecture adds a braiding to a monoidal category, derives the braid relation from its axioms, writes the axioms as equations in F- and R-symbols, and solves them for Fibonacci. Closing a braided strand gives the topological spin.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • State the braid group and why it replaces the symmetric group in a plane.
  • Define a braiding, prove the braid relation from the hexagons, and tell the braid form of the Yang–Baxter equation from Integrability’s form.
  • Write the hexagon equations in F- and R-symbols and solve them for Fibonacci.
  • Compute topological spins, and find the braidings of the pointed categories on Z2\Z_2Z2​.

Theorem 7.1 (Configuration spaces). The fundamental group of the space of nnn unordered distinct points in R2\mathbb R^{2}R2 is BnB_nBn​ cited (Kassel–Turaev §1.4.3); in Rd\mathbb R^{d}Rd with d≥3d\geq3d≥3 it is SnS_nSn​ sketched.

For d≥3d\geq3d≥3: ordered configurations form Rdn\mathbb R^{dn}Rdn with the diagonals xi=xjx_i=x_jxi​=xj​ removed, each of codimension d≥3d\geq3d≥3, and removing subspaces of codimension at least 333 leaves a space simply connected. 7.2 Braided categories and hexagons

As in Lecture 4, we work as if the category were strict.

Definition 7.2 (Braiding). A braiding on a monoidal category is a natural isomorphism cX,Y ⁣:X⊗Y→Y⊗Xc_{X,Y}\colon X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes XcX,Y​:X⊗Y→Y⊗X, drawn with the strand from bottom left to top right over the other, such that the hexagons hold: cX,Y⊗Z=(id⁡Y⊗cX,Z)∘(cX,Y⊗id⁡Z),cX⊗Y,Z=(cX,Z⊗id⁡Y)∘(id⁡X⊗cY,Z).\begin{aligned} &c_{X,Y\otimes Z}\\ &\quad=(\id_Y\otimes c_{X,Z})\circ(c_{X,Y}\otimes\id_Z),\\ &c_{X\otimes Y,Z}\\ &\quad=(c_{X,Z}\otimes\id_Y)\circ(\id_X\otimes c_{Y,Z}). \end{aligned}​cX,Y⊗Z​=(idY​⊗cX,Z​)∘(cX,Y​⊗idZ​),cX⊗Y,Z​=(cX,Z​⊗idY​)∘(idX​⊗cY,Z​).​ It is symmetric if cY,X∘cX,Y=id⁡X⊗Yc_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}=\id_{X\otimes Y}cY,X​∘cX,Y​=idX⊗Y​ for all X,YX,YX,Y.

With the associators put back, each hexagon has six sides, hence the name (EGNO Def. 8.1.1). In pictures, the first says that a strand crossing over a pair crosses each in turn, and the second that a pair crossing over a strand does so one at a time: (7.1) Naturality says that boxes slide through crossings, and invertibility that a crossing followed by its inverse is no crossing at all (Reidemeister II): (7.2)

The double braiding cY,X∘cX,Yc_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}cY,X​∘cX,Y​ takes one strand once around the other. A braiding is symmetric exactly when the double braiding is always trivial: (7.3) and then over- and under-crossings agree, and are drawn as a plain crossing.
