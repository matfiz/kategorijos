Lecture 10

Modular tensor categories and anyon models

What this lecture does

Lectures 5 to 7 and 9 built braided fusion categories with a twist: fusion rules, F- and R-symbols, spins. Which of them describe anyons in a two-dimensional medium? Physically, in a bosonic phase every anyon other than the vacuum must be detectable by braiding something around it. Categorically, this is the statement that one matrix, the Hopf link SSS, is invertible. From SSS and the spins come the Verlinde formula, which recovers the fusion rules, a projective representation of SL(2,Z)\SL(2,\Z)SL(2,Z), and a central charge ccc modulo 888. This lecture computes all of it for Fibonacci and tabulates it for the other models of the course.

10.2 Premodular, modularity, the killing loop

Lecture 7 called xxx transparent if cy,xcx,y=id⁡c_{y,x}c_{x,y}=\idcy,x​cx,y​=id for every yyy, and the transparent objects the Müger centre. A transparent xxx has λx(b)=db\lambda_x(b)=d_bλx​(b)=db​ for every bbb: its loops are those of the vacuum, so no loop around it detects it.

Definition 10.5 (Modular category, anyon model). A premodular category is modular if SSS is invertible (EGNO Def. 8.13.4). A unitary modular category is an anyon model: its simple objects are the anyon types, NNN the fusion rules, and FFF, RRR the recoupling and exchange data (Kitaev App. E).

Every gapped phase of bosons in two dimensions, with no symmetry imposed, carries an anyon model, in which only the vacuum is transparent physical principle; this is Kitaev’s “braiding nondegeneracy” (pp. 66 and 86). Fermions are excluded: a transparent fermion braids trivially with everything, yet its spin θ=−1\theta=-1θ=−1 tells it from the vacuum.

The killing loop is the loop labelled ω0=1dim⁡C∑bdb b.\omega_0=\frac1{\dim\cat{C}}\sum_bd_b\,b .ω0​=dimC1​b∑​db​b.

10.4 SL(2,Z)\SL(2,\Z)SL(2,Z) and the central charge

Theorem 10.9 (Gauss sums cited). In a modular category, the Gauss sums p±=∑ada2θa±1p_\pm=\sum_ad_a^{2}\theta_a^{\pm1}p±​=∑a​da2​θa±1​ satisfy p+p−=dim⁡Cp_+p_-=\dim\cat{C}p+​p−​=dimC (Bakalov–Kirillov Cor. 3.1.11, (3.1.20); EGNO Prop. 8.15.4). EGNO writes T=diag(θ−1)T=\mathrm{diag}(\theta^{-1})T=diag(θ−1) (Thm. 8.16.1), and with it the relation reads (ST)3=p−S2(ST)^{3}=p_-S^{2}(ST)3=p−​S2 for its unnormalised SSS: the same content in another convention. The number ccc is defined only modulo 888: the modular data see it only through e2πic/8e^{2\pi ic/8}e2πic/8. Physically it is the chiral central charge of the edge of the phase physical principle (Kitaev eq. (172)).

The geometric interpretation is a cited statement of the Reshetikhin–Turaev theory, rather than an additional consequence proved here (Simon §17.3.2; Kitaev App. E.5, eqs. (232)–(233)). A solid torus is a filled doughnut, whose boundary is a torus T2T^2T2. A core ribbon labelled by a simple object aaa prepares a boundary state ∣a⟩\lvert a\rangle∣a⟩. These states form a basis of the torus state space. In particular the toric code has four such basis states. The drawing shows the core and the two boundary cycles, followed by their images in a square with opposite sides identified.

The meridian μ\muμ goes around the tube and bounds a disk in the solid torus. The longitude λ\lambdaλ goes around the hole, parallel to the core. Orient them so that μ⋅λ=+1\mu\cdot\lambda=+1μ⋅λ=+1. The arrows in each square represent homology classes; they are not anyon worldlines or braidings. Fix the torus generators by s(μ)=λ,s(λ)=−μ,t(μ)=μ,t(λ)=λ+μ.\begin{gathered} s(\mu)=\lambda,\qquad s(\lambda)=-\mu,\\ t(\mu)=\mu,\qquad t(\lambda)=\lambda+\mu. \end{gathered}s(μ)=λ,s(λ)=−μ,t(μ)=μ,t(λ)=λ+μ.​ The map sss exchanges the two cycles with one orientation reversed. The map ttt is a Dehn twist about the meridian: cutting along that cycle, turning once and regluing adds a meridian to the longitude.

We choose the boundary and core orientations so that the basis matrix of sss is precisely the opposite-orientation Hopf-link SSS of (10.1), and the matrix of ttt is T=diag(θa)T=\mathrm{diag}(\theta_a)T=diag(θa​), up to the common scalar phases of the projective action. Thus ttt twists the core ribbon, while sss changes which cycle is filled by the meridian disk. Reversing the cycle exchange replaces sss by s−1s^{-1}s−1 and the matrix by S−1=SˉS^{-1}=\bar SS−1=Sˉ in these unitary conventions. The figure uses the displayed sss, not that inverse. This distinction is invisible in self-dual examples such as Fibonacci but matters in general. The mapping class group consists of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms up to deformation. For a torus it is SL(2,Z)\SL(2,\Z)SL(2,Z). The geometric construction and its anomaly are cited here; Lecture 14 returns to the state-space and gluing interpretation.

Remark 10.15 (The sixteen-fold way).
