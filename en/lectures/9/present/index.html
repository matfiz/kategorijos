Lecture 9

Ribbon categories, Temperley–Lieb and SU(2)k\SU(2)_kSU(2)k​

Lecture 7 found the topological spin as a closed curl, and Lecture 8 the matrix c=A−1Bqc=A^{-1}B_qc=A−1Bq​ of the braiding on two spins. This lecture puts the two together. A twist makes a braided category draw ribbons, not strings, and then every framed knot has a value. On tensor powers of one spin the calculus is the Temperley–Lieb algebra, the value is the Kauffman bracket, and its projectors are the higher spins. At a root of unity one projector vanishes in every closed picture, and discarding it leaves a finite category: SU(2)k\SU(2)_kSU(2)k​, the source of the Ising-like and Fibonacci-like anyons of Lecture 10.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Define a twist and a ribbon category, and show that the curl of Lecture 7 satisfies the twist axiom.
  • Compute with the Temperley–Lieb skein relation, and prove that the Kauffman bracket is invariant under Reidemeister II and changes by −A±3-A^{\pm3}−A±3 under Reidemeister I.
  • Construct the Jones–Wenzl projector p2p_2p2​ and compute traces and twists of coloured strands.
  • Explain how SU(2)k\SU(2)_kSU(2)k​ arises at A=eiπ/(2(k+2))A=e^{i\pi/(2(k+2))}A=eiπ/(2(k+2)) by discarding negligible morphisms and flipping the pivotal structure.

9.1 Twists and ribbon categories

Definition 9.1 (Twist, ribbon category). A twist on a braided rigid category is a natural isomorphism θX ⁣:X→X\theta_X\colon X\to XθX​:X→X with θ1=id⁡\theta_1=\idθ1​=id and θX⊗Y=cY,X cX,Y (θX⊗θY).\theta_{X\otimes Y}=c_{Y,X}\,c_{X,Y}\,(\theta_X\otimes\theta_Y).θX⊗Y​=cY,X​cX,Y​(θX​⊗θY​). It is a ribbon structure if moreover θX∗=(θX)∗\theta_{X^{*}}=(\theta_X)^{*}θX∗​=(θX​)∗ (EGNO Def. 8.10.1; Kassel Def. XIV.3.2).

The axiom is the picture of a ribbon, or a belt: a full turn of a belt holding two strands turns each strand and also wraps them once around each other, (9.1) For a simple object θa\theta_aθa​ is a number, and θa⊗b\theta_{a\otimes b}θa⊗b​ acts on the channel ccc by θc\theta_cθc​; so the axiom is the ribbon relation RcabRcba=θc/(θaθb)R^{ab}_cR^{ba}_c=\theta_c/(\theta_a\theta_b)Rcab​Rcba​=θc​/(θa​θb​) that Lecture 7 cited.

Theorem 9.2 (The curl is a twist proved given coherence). In a braided pivotal category, the curl of Lecture 7, the right partial trace of cX,Xc_{X,X}cX,X​, is a twist. In a fusion category with its canonical spherical structure, its value on a simple object is the topological spin θa\theta_aθa​ of Theorem 7.7.

Proof. Naturality: boxes slide through crossings, cups and caps. Invertibility: the curl drawn with the inverse crossing and the cap on the other side is its inverse, by RII and a snake. For X⊗YX\otimes YX⊗Y, draw the curl of the pair as a belt: the crossing cX⊗Y,X⊗Yc_{X\otimes Y,X\otimes Y}cX⊗Y,X⊗Y​ is four crossings by the hexagons, and closing the right pair of strands with the nested cup and cap of Proposition 4.3 leaves a curl on each strand and the two crossings of cY,XcX,Yc_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}cY,X​cX,Y​ between them, which is the axiom. The value on a simple object is Theorem 7.7, which assumes the canonical structure. 

In another pivotal structure the curl can differ by a sign (§9.5). In a braided fusion category the curl is a ribbon structure exactly when the pivotal structure is spherical (EGNO Prop. 8.10.12); beyond fusion categories this can fail.

So framing matters. A closed curl is not a straight strand, as it would be in a plane drawing of a knot (Reidemeister I); with a twist, framed RI holds: (9.2) The strands of a ribbon category are thin bands lying flat in the page (blackboard framing), and a curl puts a full twist into the band.

9.2 Tangles and the Reshetikhin–Turaev functor

A framed tangle is a piece of a framed oriented link in a slab, with endpoints on the bottom and top; up to isotopy, tangles form a ribbon category under stacking and juxtaposition.

Theorem 9.3 (Shum; Reshetikhin–Turaev cited). Framed oriented tangles form the free ribbon category on one object: for every object VVV of a strict ribbon category C\cat{C}C there is a unique strict ribbon functor from tangles to C\cat{C}C sending an upward strand to VVV (Kassel Thm. XIV.5.1).

A framed link is a tangle from nothing to nothing, so its image is an endomorphism of 111, a number when End⁡(1)=C\End(1)=\CEnd(1)=C, as in a fusion category: a link invariant for every object of such a ribbon category. The pictures of these lectures are honest because of this theorem.

Why is the quotient semisimple? Each coloured strand n≤kn\leq kn≤k has non-zero trace, so its identity is not negligible and it stays a simple object; the morphisms that would make the category non-semisimple, through pk+1p_{k+1}pk+1​ and beyond, are negligible and are set to zero. The traces that survive are the dimensions above.
