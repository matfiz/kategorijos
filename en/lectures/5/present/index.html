Lecture 5

Fusion categories: particles and how they fuse

Lectures 1–4 built the language: semisimple categories, tensor products, duals and loops. This lecture puts them together into the setting of anyon models. A fusion category has finitely many particle types. Two particles fuse into a sum of types, counted by multiplicities NabcN_{ab}^{c}Nabc​. States with a fixed total charge form fusion spaces. For repeated Fibonacci particles with total charge τ\tauτ, their dimensions are Fibonacci numbers. We also count even numbers of Ising particles with vacuum charge.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • State the definition of a fusion category and give the fusion rules of VecG\VecGVecG​, Rep(S3)\Rep(S_3)Rep(S3​), Fibonacci, Ising and SU(2)k\SU(2)_kSU(2)k​.
  • Prove that the multiplicities form an associative fusion ring with Nabc=Nbc∗a∗N_{ab}^{c}=N_{bc^{*}}^{a^{*}}Nabc​=Nbc∗a∗​.
  • Compute Frobenius–Perron and quantum dimensions and the global dimension.
  • Use the trivalent vertex, the bubble and completeness relations, and count fusion spaces with fusion trees.

5.1 The definition

Definition 5.1 (Fusion category). A fusion category is a C\CC-linear semisimple category with finitely many simple objects (Definition 2.11), with a monoidal structure whose tensor product is bilinear on morphisms, which is rigid (Definition 4.1), and whose unit 111 is simple. We take it pivotal (Definition 4.6), as all our examples are; then X∗∗≅XX^{**}\cong XX∗∗≅X.

The simple objects a,b,c,…a,b,c,\dotsa,b,c,… are the particle types, or anyons; 111 is the vacuum, and the dual a∗a^{*}a∗, which we also write aˉ\bar aaˉ, is the antiparticle. The tensor product of two simple objects decomposes, a⊗b≅⨁cNabc c,a\otimes b\cong\bigoplus_c N_{ab}^{c}\,c,a⊗b≅c⨁​Nabc​c, with fusion multiplicities Nabc=dim⁡Hom⁡(a⊗b,c)N_{ab}^{c}=\dim\Hom(a\otimes b,c)Nabc​=dimHom(a⊗b,c) (Lemma 2.12).

Rigidity means that every particle can be created from the vacuum together with its antiparticle, by coeva\mathrm{coev}_acoeva​, and annihilated with it, by eva\mathrm{ev}_aeva​. Rep(SU(2))\Rep(\SU(2))Rep(SU(2)) has every property but finiteness, since its spins j=0,12,1,…j=0,\tfrac12,1,\dotsj=0,21​,1,… do not stop. The category Vectfd\VectfdVectfd​ is the trivial fusion category, whose only particle is the vacuum.

Summary

  • Handed on: a fusion category is semisimple, rigid and pivotal, with finitely many simples, a simple unit and bilinear ⊗\otimes⊗ (Definition 5.1); Fibonacci, Ising, SU(2)k\SU(2)_kSU(2)k​, VecGω\VecG[\omega]VecGω​ and Rep(G)\Rep(G)Rep(G) are the examples.
  • The fusion ring: NabcN_{ab}^{c}Nabc​ form an associative ring with Nab1=δb,a∗N_{ab}^{1}=\delta_{b,a^{*}}Nab1​=δb,a∗​ and Nabc=Nbc∗a∗N_{ab}^{c}=N_{bc^{*}}^{a^{*}}Nabc​=Nbc∗a∗​ (Theorem 5.3); it does not determine the category.
  • Dimensions: FPdim\mathrm{FPdim}FPdim is the Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue, φ\varphiφ for τ\tauτ and 2\sqrt22​ for σ\sigmaσ; for a pseudo-unitary C\cat{C}C with its canonical spherical structure d=FPdimd=\mathrm{FPdim}d=FPdim, and dim⁡C=∑ada2\dim\cat{C}=\sum_ad_a^{2}dimC=∑a​da2​ (Theorems 5.4 and 5.5).
  • Pictures: the trivalent vertex with the bubble dadb/dc\sqrt{d_ad_b/d_c}da​db​/dc​​ and completeness (5.1), (5.3); admissibly labelled trees of one fixed shape form a basis, with Fibonacci numbers for τ\tauτ (Theorem 5.6).
