  1. Exercise 1 The toric-code boundaries on the lattice

    (i) Draw a piece of the lattice with a smooth boundary on top and a rough one below. On it draw a string of ZZZ’s from a bulk vertex sss down to the end of a dangling edge, and a string of XXX’s on the edges crossed by a dual path from a bulk face ppp up through the smooth boundary. Show that each string violates only the term at its bulk end.

    (ii) Show that a ZZZ-string from sss that ends at a vertex of the smooth boundary violates the star there too, and that an XXX-string from ppp that leaves through the rough boundary violates a three-edge plaquette. What does ε\varepsilonε, brought to each boundary, leave behind?

    (iii) The dual lattice has a vertex in each face and an edge across each edge. Show that passing to it, and exchanging XXX with ZZZ, turns stars into plaquettes and back, the smooth boundary into a rough one, and eee into mmm.

    Show a hint

    (i) A ZZZ anticommutes with AsA_sAs​ exactly when its edge ends at sss. (iii) What is the dual edge of an edge that lies in only one face?

    Show the answer

    (i) Each interior vertex (face) of the path meets two of its edges; the dangling end and the outer side have no term. (ii) The boundary star (three edges) meets one edge of the string, the truncated plaquette one; ε\varepsilonε leaves eee on the smooth boundary and mmm on the rough one. (iii) An edge in one face becomes a dangling dual edge, so smooth becomes rough and eee (a star) becomes mmm (a plaquette).

    Show the worked method

    (i) In the picture the ZZZ-string runs down the dashed vertical path from sss, the XXX-string on the edges crossed by the dotted dual path from ppp: A product of ZZZ’s on a set bbb of edges anticommutes with AvA_vAv​ exactly when an odd number of edges of bbb end at vvv. Along the path every vertex other than sss meets two of its edges, and the lower end of the dangling edge is no vertex (it has no star), so only AsA_sAs​ is violated: the bulk eee at sss remains, and the end on the rough boundary leaves no excitation. Likewise a product of XXX’s anticommutes with BfB_fBf​ exactly when an odd number of its edges lie on ∂f\partial f∂f; along the dual path every face other than ppp contains two crossed edges, and the top boundary edge lies in one face only, so only BpB_pBp​ is violated: mmm is absorbed at the smooth boundary.

    (ii) A ZZZ-string ending at a vertex vvv of the top row meets vvv in one edge, so AvA_vAv​ (a three-edge star) is violated: eee arrives at the boundary as an excitation. An XXX-string leaving through the bottom crosses the horizontal edge of a three-edge plaquette, which it meets once, so that plaquette is violated. So ε=e⊗m\varepsilon=e\otimes mε=e⊗m brought to the smooth boundary leaves eee, and to the rough boundary mmm: the maps 1,m↦11,m\mapsto11,m↦1, e,ε↦ee,\varepsilon\mapsto ee,ε↦e and 1,e↦11,e\mapsto11,e↦1, m,ε↦mm,\varepsilon\mapsto mm,ε↦m of Lecture 13.

    (iii) Dual vertices are faces, dual edges cross edges, and a dual face surrounds a vertex; the edges around a dual face are those crossing the edges at that vertex. So, with X↔ZX\leftrightarrow ZX↔Z, the star Av=∏XA_v=\prod XAv​=∏X on the edges at vvv becomes ∏Z\prod Z∏Z around the dual face at vvv, a plaquette, and BfB_fBf​ becomes a star at the dual vertex fff: the Hamiltonian is the toric code again. A top edge lies in one face, so its dual edge has one end, at that face, and dangles: the smooth boundary becomes rough, and its three-edge stars become three-edge plaquettes. A violated star, eee, becomes a violated plaquette, mmm. This is the lattice face of the exchange e↔me\leftrightarrow me↔m of Sheet 12, Exercise 4: it carries one boundary to the other.
