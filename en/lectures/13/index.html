Lecture 13

Boundaries, defects and non-invertible symmetries

What this lecture does

Lectures 11 and 12 built module categories, dual categories and the centre as mathematics. This lecture interprets them physically. The toric code has rough and smooth lattice boundaries, corresponding to the two module categories over VecZ2\catname{Vec}_{\Z_2}VecZ2​​. The anyons absorbed at each boundary form a Lagrangian algebra. In the critical Ising field theory, an interface between the theory and its gauged version becomes a topological line NNN. Its fusion rule N⊗N=1⊕ηN\otimes N=1\oplus\etaN⊗N=1⊕η makes this symmetry non-invertible. Gauging a group symmetry means summing over networks of its symmetry lines. Full gauging uses a module category with one simple object, which exists only when the symmetry has no anomaly.

13.1 The toric-code boundaries

Recall the toric code of Quantum Computing (Lecture 14): qubits on the edges of a square lattice, the star term As=∏XA_s=\prod XAs​=∏X on the four edges at a vertex and the plaquette term Bp=∏ZB_p=\prod ZBp​=∏Z around a face. A string of ZZZ’s on a path violates the stars at its two ends, creating eee’s; a string of XXX’s crossing a path on the dual lattice violates the plaquettes at its ends, creating mmm’s. In Lecture 12 these are the simple objects e=(0,χ−)e=(0,\chi_-)e=(0,χ−​) and m=(1,1)m=(1,1)m=(1,1) of Z(VecZ2)\mathcal{Z}(\catname{Vec}_{\Z_2})Z(VecZ2​​). These pairs use additive group labels: 000 is the identity and 111 is the generator previously called sss.

A Hamiltonian is gapped if the energy gap above its ground states is bounded below by a constant independent of the system size (Kitaev and Kong, fn. 1). A boundary ends the lattice along a line, and its terms are the truncated stars and plaquettes that remain; it is a gapped boundary if the Hamiltonian with it stays gapped. There are two natural ways to cut:

The thick green edges support one boundary star. The shaded three-edge plaquettes B−B_-B−​ and B+B_+B+​ share the dangling edge carrying the ZZZ-string endpoint. The dashed XXX-string exits through the smooth boundary. At the smooth boundary (top) the lattice ends along a row of edges, each in one plaquette, and the boundary stars have three edges. At the rough boundary (bottom) edges stick out, each meeting one vertex, and the boundary plaquettes have three edges (Kitaev–Kong §2; Bravyi and Kitaev call the rough one an xxx-boundary and the smooth one a zzz-boundary, and the names vary between sources).

Theorem 13.1 (The toric-code boundaries). At the smooth boundary mmm condenses: an XXX-string ending on the boundary leaves no excitation there, while eee cannot be absorbed. At the rough boundary eee condenses and mmm cannot be absorbed. The excitations that remain are {1,e}\{1,e\}{1,e} on the smooth boundary and {1,m}\{1,m\}{1,m} on the rough one.

Proof. Absorption is local: consider operators supported near one long boundary, away from its ends and from the other boundary. An XXX on an edge flips the plaquettes containing it, a ZZZ flips the stars at its ends, and every operator is a sum of products of these. A smooth-boundary edge lies in one plaquette, so an XXX-string on the dual lattice that ends by crossing it violates only the plaquette at its other end: mmm is absorbed. But every edge there still has two ends, each with a star, so every product of Paulis supported there flips an even number of stars: the number of eee’s keeps its parity, and a single eee cannot disappear. At the rough boundary the outer end of a dangling edge has no star, so a ZZZ-string ending there violates nothing at that end: eee is absorbed. Every edge, the dangling ones included, lies in two plaquettes, so the same parity count keeps a single mmm. 

So the two boundaries are told apart by which anyon disappears into them: mmm on the smooth one, eee on the rough one (Kong–Zhang Rem. 4.1.1 calls them the mmm- and eee-condensed boundaries). Bringing any bulk anyon to the boundary gives a boundary excitation, and fusion decides which. At the smooth boundary mmm becomes 111, so ε=e⊗m\varepsilon=e\otimes mε=e⊗m becomes eee: the map is 1,m↦11,m\mapsto11,m↦1 and e,ε↦ee,\varepsilon\mapsto ee,ε↦e. At the rough boundary it is 1,e↦11,e\mapsto11,e↦1 and m,ε↦mm,\varepsilon\mapsto mm,ε↦m (Kitaev–Kong, maps (3)–(4)). The boundary excitations fuse like Z2\Z_2Z2​, but they are not anyons: on a line they cannot braid.

13.2 Module categories and boundaries; Lagrangian algebras

The anyons that condense on a boundary form an algebra in the braided centre, and the algebra must be of a special kind.

For VecZ2\catname{Vec}_{\Z_2}VecZ2​​, the regular module (H=1H=1H=1) has dual VecZ2\catname{Vec}_{\Z_2}VecZ2​​, and the module Vectfd\VectfdVectfd​ (H=Z2H=\Z_2H=Z2​) has dual Rep(Z2)\Rep(\Z_2)Rep(Z2​) (Theorem 12.2). A topological line in a field theory is an operator supported on a curve in spacetime whose small deformations change no correlation function away from the other insertions; along a spatial circle it commutes with the Hamiltonian (Shao §2.2).

To gauge a finite symmetry, we sum over insertions of networks of its lines, joined by the multiplication of an algebra in the symmetry category. The next section states when this construction is possible for the whole group.

13.4 Full gauging, Rep(G), anomaly (statement)

Definition 13.8 (Full gauging, anomaly). Full gauging uses the symmetry lines of the whole group in the network construction above. A fusion category is anomalous if it has no module category with one simple object (Thorngren and Wang, p. 13). By Theorem 11.6 with H=GH=GH=G, VecGω\VecG[\omega]VecGω​ is anomalous exactly when [ω]≠0[\omega]\neq0[ω]=0.

For Z2\Z_2Z2​ this is Kramers–Wannier again: gauging η\etaη gives a theory with symmetry Rep(Z2)≃VecZ2\Rep(\Z_2)\simeq\catname{Vec}_{\Z_2}Rep(Z2​)≃VecZ2​​. Gauging a subgroup uses the other module categories, beyond this course.

Summary

  • Boundaries: the toric code has the smooth (mmm condenses, excitations {1,e}\{1,e\}{1,e}) and the rough (eee condenses, excitations {1,m}\{1,m\}{1,m}) lattice boundaries (Theorem 13.1); by the physical principle they are its only gapped ones.
  • Lagrangian algebras: connected étale LLL with d(L)2=dim⁡Zd(L)^{2}=\dim\mathcal{Z}d(L)2=dimZ; in the toric code exactly 1⊕e1\oplus e1⊕e and 1⊕m1\oplus m1⊕m (Theorem 13.3); they match the module categories, and the excitations are the duals with the tensor product reversed (Theorems 13.4 and 13.5).
  • Non-invertible symmetry: the critical Ising field theory has the line NNN with N⊗N=1⊕ηN\otimes N=1\oplus\etaN⊗N=1⊕η, the category TY(Z2,χ,+1/2)\mathrm{TY}(\Z_2,\chi,+1/\sqrt2)TY(Z2​,χ,+1/2​) (Theorem 13.7); on the periodic chain D2=(1+η)T−1D^{2}=(1+\eta)\mathcal{T}^{-1}D2=(1+η)T−1 instead.
  • Gauging: full gauging of VecGω\VecG[\omega]VecGω​ needs [ω]=0[\omega]=0[ω]=0 and, for ω=ψ=1\omega=\psi=1ω=ψ=1, yields Rep(G)\Rep(G)Rep(G) (Theorem 13.9).
