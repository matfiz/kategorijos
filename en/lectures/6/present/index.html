Lecture 6

F-symbols and the pentagon

Lecture 5 gave each anyon model its particles and fusion rules, and a basis of fusion trees for the states of many particles. It said nothing about the associator, which Lecture 3 made part of the definition. In a fusion category the associator is a finite table of numbers, the F-symbols: the change of basis between two bracketings of three particles. Mac Lane’s pentagon becomes a system of polynomial equations for them. This lecture writes it down and solves it for Fibonacci, where the fusion rules leave almost no freedom.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Define the F-symbols on fusion trees and prove their unitarity for a unitary category with normalised vertices.
  • Derive the pentagon equation from Mac Lane’s pentagon.
  • Compute how a change of vertices acts on the F-symbols, and find gauge invariants among them.
  • Solve the pentagon for Fibonacci, and verify the two Ising solutions and why they are inequivalent.

6.2 The pentagon equation

Four particles a,b,c,da,b,c,da,b,c,d with total charge eee have five bracketings, and the F-moves connect them. Labelling the inner edges as in the picture,

the two paths from the top-left tree to a(b(cd))a(b(cd))a(b(cd)) on the right must agree.

Theorem 6.3 (The pentagon equation). The F-symbols of C\cat{C}C satisfy, for all labels, [Fefcd]gl [Feabl]fk=∑h [Fgabc]fh [Feahd]gk [Fkbcd]hl.\begin{aligned} &[F^{fcd}_e]_{gl}\,[F^{abl}_e]_{fk}\\ &\quad=\sum_h\,[F^{abc}_g]_{fh}\,[F^{ahd}_e]_{gk}\,[F^{bcd}_k]_{hl} . \end{aligned}​[Fefcd​]gl​[Feabl​]fk​=h∑​[Fgabc​]fh​[Feahd​]gk​[Fkbcd​]hl​.​ Conversely sketched, numbers [Fdabc]ef[F^{abc}_d]_{ef}[Fdabc​]ef​ for a fusion ring, invertible and equal to 111 on unit legs, that satisfy these equations define an associator which makes the skeletal category with these fusion rules monoidal.

Proof. Start from the tree ((ab)c)d((ab)c)d((ab)c)d with channels fff for ababab and ggg for (ab)c(ab)c(ab)c. The upper path first applies the F-move to fff, ccc, ddd, which changes ggg into lll, the channel of cdcdcd, with coefficient [Fefcd]gl[F^{fcd}_e]_{gl}[Fefcd​]gl​; then the F-move to aaa, bbb, lll, which changes fff into kkk, with coefficient [Feabl]fk[F^{abl}_e]_{fk}[Feabl​]fk​. This reaches the tree a(b(cd))a(b(cd))a(b(cd)) with channels lll and kkk. The lower path applies FgabcF^{abc}_gFgabc​ inside, changing fff into hhh, the channel of bcbcbc; then FeahdF^{ahd}_eFeahd​, changing ggg into kkk; then FkbcdF^{bcd}_kFkbcd​, changing hhh into lll. The two paths are the two sides of Mac Lane’s pentagon (Definition 3.1), applied to the starting tree, so they are equal; the trees a(b(cd))a(b(cd))a(b(cd)) are a basis (Theorem 5.6, mirrored), so their coefficients are equal, which is the equation.

For the converse, define α\alphaα on simple objects by the F-symbols and extend it by linearity. Mac Lane’s pentagon then needs checking only on simple objects, where it is the equation, and the triangle holds by the unit legs. This is the argument of Theorem 3.8.

6.3 Gauge and Ocneanu

The vertices were chosen up to a phase (Lecture 5), and the F-symbols depend on the choice; any non-zero rescaling of the vertices changes them in the same way.

The pentagon solutions for fixed fusion rules are rigid.

Theorem 6.5 (Ocneanu rigidity cited). A fusion category admits no non-trivial deformations, and there are only finitely many fusion categories with a given fusion ring, up to equivalence (EGNO Thm. 9.1.4).

In F-symbols: the pentagon for a given fusion ring has only finitely many solutions up to gauge transformations with u≠0u\neq0u=0 (Bonderson, p. 33).

  • Handed on: the F-symbols are the associator on fusion trees, α∘Le=∑f[Fdabc]efRf\alpha\circ L_e=\sum_f[F^{abc}_d]_{ef}R_fα∘Le​=∑f​[Fdabc​]ef​Rf​. For a unitary category with a norm-preserving vertex gauge, the admissible F-matrices are unitary. Entries on allowed unit legs are 111 (Definition 6.1 and Proposition 6.2).
  • The pentagon: Mac Lane’s pentagon is a polynomial system in the F-symbols (Theorem 6.3); for VecGω\VecG[\omega]VecGω​ it is the cocycle condition.
  • Gauge: changing vertices rescales F (Theorem 6.4); only invariants, such as κa=da[Faaaa]11\kappa_a=d_a[F^{aaa}_a]_{11}κa​=da​[Faaaa​]11​, are physics. Ocneanu: finitely many solutions up to gauge (Theorem 6.5).
  • Fibonacci: Fττττ=(φ−1φ−1/2φ−1/2−φ−1)F^{\tau\tau\tau}_\tau=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} \varphi^{-1}&\varphi^{-1/2}\\\varphi^{-1/2}&-\varphi^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)Fττττ​=(φ−1φ−1/2​φ−1/2−φ−1​), unique up to gauge among unitary solutions; Yang–Lee is its non-unitary conjugate (Theorem 6.6).
